# Guided Mission — Student Portfolio Starter

## Mission

Launch a professional technical portfolio that will collect evidence of your work in CIT-205 and continue growing through future CIT and SOTE coursework.

This is not a web-development assignment. GitHub and GitHub Pages are the tools we will use to publish evidence of work you have already performed.

## Mission Objective

By the end of this guided mission, you will have:

- your own portfolio repository;
- a live GitHub Pages website;
- a professional introduction;
- the required CIT-205 portfolio sections;
- at least one sanitized evidence entry; and
- a structure that can grow throughout this course and future courses.

## Portfolio Sections

Your starter portfolio will include:

1. **Home / About** — professional introduction, interests, and goals.
2. **Missions** — mission proposals and completed mission work.
3. **Tickets** — sanitized support cases and ticketing evidence.
4. **Knowledge Base** — articles you authored or substantially revised.
5. **Validation & Troubleshooting** — workstation validation, troubleshooting cases, and reflections.
6. **Skills & Evidence** — course capabilities connected to actual artifacts.

## Evidence Pattern

For each portfolio artifact, use this structure:

**Context → Action → Evidence → Result / Reflection**

The goal is not to claim that you have a skill. The goal is to show evidence that you used it.

## Guided Build

### Step 1 — Create Your Portfolio Repository

Open [**Use this template**](https://github.com/student-operated-technology-ecosystem/cit205-portfolio-template/generate). Select **your own account** as Owner, choose **Public**, and name the repository `YOUR-USERNAME.github.io` using your exact GitHub username. For example, `alexmorgan` creates `alexmorgan.github.io`. Leave **Include all branches** unchecked and select **Create repository**.

The template places `index.html`, `style.css`, and `artifacts/` directly in your repository root. If you already have a `YOUR-USERNAME.github.io` repository, preserve it and copy the starter files into it without overwriting existing work.

Follow the [ACE Portfolio Publication Mission](ACE-portfolio-publication-mission.md) for the complete publishing walkthrough.

### Step 2 — Personalize the Starter

Update the starter with:

- your name;
- a short professional introduction;
- your technical interests;
- your current goals; and
- CIT-205 / ACE Support Command as the source of the initial evidence.

### Step 3 — Add Your First Artifact

Choose one appropriate piece of CIT-205 evidence. Good starting choices include:

- your ACE Mission Proposal;
- a sanitized ticket;
- a Knowledge Article;
- a workstation-validation reflection; or
- another instructor-approved artifact.

Describe it using **Context → Action → Evidence → Result / Reflection**.

### Step 4 — Commit the Change

Commit your work with a short professional message that explains what changed.

Example:

`Add first CIT-205 mission evidence`

### Step 5 — Publish With GitHub Pages

In your repository:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Open **Pages**.
3. Under **Build and deployment → Source**, select **Deploy from a branch**.
4. Select the `main` branch and **/(root)**.
5. Select **Save**.
6. Wait several minutes for deployment, refresh **Settings → Pages**, and select **Visit site**.
7. Your live portfolio URL is `https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io/`. Your repository URL is `https://github.com/YOUR-USERNAME/YOUR-USERNAME.github.io`. Submit both to Blackboard. Commits to `main` automatically republish the site.

### Step 6 — Validate the Site

Open the published site and verify:

- navigation works;
- your name and introduction are correct;
- the first artifact is readable;
- the site works on a phone-sized screen; and
- no sensitive information has been published.

## Publishing Boundary

Your portfolio is public professional evidence. Never publish:

- passwords, tokens, or credentials;
- private URLs;
- personally identifiable information;
- customer or student records;
- private ticket information;
- sensitive infrastructure details; or
- screenshots containing information that should not be public.

Use sanitized, redacted, fictionalized, or synthetic evidence when necessary.

## Definition of Done

You are finished with the starter mission when:

- the repository exists;
- GitHub Pages is live;
- the required sections exist;
- your professional introduction is present;
- at least one evidence entry is published safely; and
- you can provide the instructor with both the repository URL and the live portfolio URL.

## Where This Goes Next

This is a living portfolio. CIT-205 establishes the foundation. Future networking, server, cybersecurity, hardware, and SOTE work can be added as new evidence. Later in the semester, you will use this evidence to develop stronger résumé bullets and LinkedIn content.
