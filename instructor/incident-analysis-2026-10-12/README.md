## Evidence and videos

- A: October 4 Sepang wet-weather power-unit software. [FIA investigation](https://www.fia.com/news/fia-concludes-investigation-bahrain-gp-malaysia-formation-lap-software-bug). The original package underwent simulation and FP1 testing. A low-RPM wet-condition interaction with a 250 kW sector reduction exposed an operating condition not adequately covered. Do not describe the original package as entirely untested. The response timeline is reproduced in the slides using the times reported by the FIA; no timezone is inferred.
- B: October 9 Singapore Sprint Qualifying. [RACER reporting quoting the FIA statement](https://racer.com/2026/10/09/fom-and-fia-issues-combine-to-disrupt-sprint-qualifying-in-singapore). FOM timing and the separate Race Control Overtake Mode state error require distinct evidence and recovery validation. The 27-second activation created uneven availability. The public reporting does not establish the technical root cause of the timing fault.
- C: October 11 Verstappen radio. [Official FIA post-race transcript](https://api.fia.com/news/f1-2026-singapore-grand-prix-post-race-press-conference-transcript) and [official F1 radio video](https://www.formula1.com/en/video/2026-singapore-grand-prix-red-bull-forced-to-improvise-after-verstappen-radio-problems.1878767102419578531). ## Student qualification

Record expected and actual behavior; assess impact, urgency and affected scope; cite a timeline; distinguish facts, reports, hypotheses and proposed actions; evaluate response, authority, escalation, change risk and validation; explain incident versus problem work; provide an actionable knowledge handoff. Address all eleven existing review sections and at least three ITIL practices. If no patch is documented, evaluate the actual workaround and label any proposed change. Do not invent a software patch for the radio case.
